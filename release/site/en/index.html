01 · Check

Check your site

Enter your site's address. We read up to 10 public pages as a new visitor would, without cookies, and tell you what starts before consent.

Domain only, no paths or parameters.

We keep neither the address nor the result. Details in Privacy.

What we cannot see from here

The check reads the code of the homepage and of other pages, up to 10 pages in all. It does not see:

  • the pages after the first 10;
  • what Tag Manager or other scripts load after the page starts;
  • cookies set from JavaScript;
  • trackers in event attributes (onload, onclick), in iframe srcdoc or in <noscript>;
  • addresses built from pieces of text or encoded;
  • what the site does with the data and what its policy says.

"No problems found" only means we found nothing on the pages we checked. It is not a guarantee that the site follows the law.

Check it yourself in 5 minutes

  1. Open a private (incognito) window in Chrome or Firefox. You start with no cookies and no saved consent.
  2. Open DevTools (F12, or right-click and "Inspect"), go to the Network tab and tick "Disable cache".
  3. Open the site. Do not click anything in the banner.
  4. In the Network filter, type your services' domains one by one, for example google-analytics.com, googletagmanager.com, facebook.net, clarity.ms. No request to them should show up.
  5. In the Application tab (Storage in Firefox), under Cookies, there should be no _ga, _fbp, _clck or other tracking cookies.
  6. Click "Accept" in the banner. Now the requests and cookies should appear. That shows blocking holds exactly until consent.

02 · The law

What the law asks

In Moldova, the cookie rule comes from two laws. Law No. 72/2025 on electronic communications, art. 116, in force since 1 January 2026, says when you may store or read anything on a visitor's device. Law No. 195/2024 on personal data protection, in force since 23 August 2026, says what consent must look like and what you must be able to prove. In short:

  1. Consent first, not after. Analytics and marketing start only after the visitor says yes. Art. 116 itself asks for information; consent comes from Law 195/2024 as soon as the cookie identifies the visitor. Law 195/2024 art. 6-7, Law 72/2025 art. 116
  2. Say clearly, beforehand, what you collect and why. Clear and complete information about the purposes, before any cookie that is not strictly necessary. Law 72/2025 art. 116(5), Law 195/2024 art. 12-13
  3. Strictly necessary needs no consent. Session, cart, security and the cookie that remembers the choice work without asking. Law 72/2025 art. 116(6)
  4. You can prove consent. You must be able to show the visitor consented. Law 195/2024 art. 7(1)
  5. Withdrawing is as easy as consenting. Visitors can change their mind at any time, just as simply. Law 195/2024 art. 7(3)
  6. Not through "I accept the terms". Consent given by accepting the terms and conditions as a whole is not valid. Law 195/2024 art. 7, CNPDCP practice
  7. EU visitors call for the EU standard. For them the GDPR and their country's law (in Romania, Law 506/2004) apply directly, and both require explicit consent. GDPR art. 3(2)

Fines under Law 195/2024 go up to MDL 1,000,000 or 1% of turnover, and up to MDL 2,000,000 or 2% for serious breaches (art. 86-88). They are phased in: 10% of the fine in the first year, 40% in the second, 100% from the third (art. 90(4)).

03 · The plugin

What the plugin does

  • Blocks until consent

    Scripts and embeds (YouTube, Vimeo, Google Maps) stay off until the visitor chooses. With Google Consent Mode v2 and a Strict or Advanced measurement mode.

  • Knows the services

    A library of 18 services, from Google Analytics and Meta Pixel to Hotjar and Tawk.to, blocked from day one. You add what is missing.

  • Scans and checks

    Scans up to 20 pages of your site daily and tests in the browser what loads before and after consent. It also shows resources that send the visitor's IP address.

  • Legal texts for Moldova

    Cookie policy, privacy policy and terms in Romanian, Russian and English, citing Law 195/2024, Law 72/2025 and the GDPR. The cookie table builds itself.

  • Keeps the proof

    A consent log in your own database, with an ID per visitor. The IP address is kept only as a salted hash. CSV and JSON export.

  • Works for everyone

    Accessible to WCAG 2.2 AA: contrast, visible focus, the Escape key, 24 px targets, reduced motion. On phones, a two-line bar.

  • Looks like your site

    Three styles, your colours, bar, card or modal. Whatever you pick, "Accept" and "Reject" stay equally visible: same style, same size.

  • Signed updates

    Every update is signed with Ed25519 and checked by your site before it installs. No telemetry: the update check carries nothing about your site.

06 · External resources

Google Fonts and other resources that send the IP address

When a page loads a font from Google, the visitor's browser connects to Google's servers and sends them its IP address. Nobody asked the visitor. The IP address is personal data.

In January 2022 a court in Munich (Landgericht München I, case 3 O 17493/20) ruled that a site loading Google Fonts from Google's servers breached the GDPR, because the fonts can also be used locally without the visitor's IP address leaving. The site had to pay €100 in damages to the visitor who complained. It is a German court's decision and is not law in Moldova, but it shows how the GDPR is read in the EU.

The same happens with Adobe Fonts, Font Awesome, public CDNs, Gravatar avatars and WordPress emoji.

What the plugin does

  • Finds them: the scan shows, page by page, the "Resources that send the IP address": Google Fonts, Adobe Fonts, Font Awesome, CDNs, Gravatar, WordPress emoji.
  • Moves Google Fonts to your server: you turn on "Localize Google Fonts" in Settings, click "Localize fonts" and your server downloads the fonts once. From then on visitors no longer contact Google.
  • Turns off emoji and Gravatar: two switches in Settings.
  • You move the rest: for Adobe Fonts, Font Awesome and CDNs the plugin shows you where they are, and you host the files on your server.

08 · Install

Running in 5 minutes

  1. Download consent-manager-pro.zip. In WordPress, under Plugins, upload the zip file and activate the plugin.
  2. Settings: keep Pro mode (the banner with consent per category). Keep "Block tracking scripts until consent" ticked and the measurement mode on Strict.
  3. Services: look at the "Found on the site" list. The plugin scans on its own. Make sure nothing you know the site uses is missing.
  4. Legal pages: fill in the operator's details and click "Generate" for each document. Read the pages and publish them.
  5. The "Cookie settings" link in the footer, with the [cmp_cookie_settings_link] shortcode. Visitors use it to change or withdraw consent.
  6. Check: click "Check in browser". No page should come out red.

The full guide is inside the plugin, under Consent Manager, "Guide". Not on WordPress? Use the standalone version.

09 · Questions

Frequently asked questions

Why is it free?

Because we want every site in Moldova to be able to ask for consent properly without paying for it. The code is public, under the GPL. There is no licence key, no subscription and no hidden features.

Does "Pro" mean there is a paid version?

No. "Pro" means professional. There is no paid version and no paid service.

Does it work without WordPress?

Yes. For static sites there is the standalone version: two files, cmp.js and cmp.css. You mark the scripts that wait for consent yourself. It keeps no consent log.

What data reaches RT?

From the plugin, only the update check: your site downloads a static manifest from consent.rt.md, without the site's address and without visitor data. From the check on this page: the address you type, used only for the check and not kept. It is all in Privacy.

Is the plugin enough to make my site follow the law?

Not on its own. The plugin does the banner, the blocking, the consent log and starting texts. Still up to you: the record of processing activities, a data protection officer where required, data transfers abroad and the final wording of the legal texts. Ask a lawyer about those.

What about Google Tag Manager?

The plugin blocks what is in the page code. In Strict mode, Tag Manager starts only after consent. What Tag Manager loads after it starts is controlled from Tag Manager, through Google Consent Mode, which the plugin sends.

Which versions does it support?

WordPress 6.0 or later and PHP 7.4 or later. It also works on multisite.

How do I report a security issue?

Write to security@rt.md. Details on the Security page.
